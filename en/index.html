Artificial intelligence

AI that listens, understands, checks and learns.

In Chad, many people would rather speak than write. SahelVoice puts speech recognition to work for surveys, with a person to check whatever the machine is not sure it understood.

01

Listens

A speech recognition model (Whisper) transcribes each voice note within seconds, on our own servers.

audio 0:04
→ « nous sommes sept à la maison »
02

Understands

It pulls out the expected answer: a number said in words, a yes or a no, a choice from the list, a rating from 1 to 5.

« je donne quatre »
→ service_rating = 4
03

Checks

A spoken number is read back to the person to confirm. An answer it cannot understand is asked again once, then sent to your enumerators.

“You said: 7. Is that right?”
→ Yes ✓
04

Learns

Every enumerator correction measures the real transcription accuracy. With separate consent from respondents, it helps train our models for the languages of Chad.

« cette » corrected to « sept »
→ error rate measured

We do not publish an accuracy figure until we have measured it on real answers from Chad. The dashboard computes that figure from your own enumerators' checks.

The platform

The whole survey cycle, in one place.

From importing the questionnaire to the final export, SahelVoice replaces paper forms and one-by-one phone calls.

Your questionnaire as it is

Upload the XLSForm file from KoBoToolbox or ODK. Simple skip logic is kept; anything WhatsApp cannot do is flagged clearly.

Invitations and QR codes

Invite a contact list, or put up a keyword and a QR code. The person writes in, and the survey starts.

Live results

Each answer reaches the dashboard the moment it is sent, with the audio ready to play.

Review queue

Your enumerators listen to voice notes one by one: correct, fix, or inaudible. The next one opens by itself.

Analysis-ready export

One row per person with the checked values, in Excel or CSV. Audio in a ZIP, named like the columns.

Team and roles

Human + AI

Research

The science behind SahelVoice.

SahelVoice builds on published research in natural language processing for low-resource African languages, starting with Ngambay, one of the most widely spoken languages in southern Chad.

01

Data where there was none

The first Ngambay–French parallel corpus, released publicly for research.

02

Models adapted to Chad

Pre-trained translation models, fine-tuned on that corpus for a language they had never seen.

03

Towards voice

The model already translates questionnaires into Ngambay, in testing, and we keep improving it. The same approach guides the speech recognition we are preparing.

Data protection

Respondents stay in control.

Consent first

Asked before the first question. Without consent, nothing is recorded and the person appears nowhere.

STOP means stop

Writing STOP ends all messages, from every organisation, for good.

Phone numbers hidden

Phone numbers are replaced by a code in exports and hidden in the dashboard.

Audio deleted on time

Voice notes are erased after the period the organisation sets. Reusing them for research needs a second, separate consent.

FAQ

Questions we often hear.

Do respondents need to install an app?

No. Everything happens in WhatsApp, which people already use. They send a keyword, scan a QR code or reply to an invitation, and the survey starts.

Do people need to be able to write?

No. Questions arrive as text, but people can answer by tapping a button or sending a voice note. AI transcribes the voice and your enumerators check it.

Can we use our KoBo or ODK questionnaire?

Yes. Upload your XLSForm file as it is. Questions, choices and simple skip logic carry over. Anything WhatsApp cannot do, such as a photo or a GPS point, is flagged before you send.

Which languages are supported?

French today, written and spoken. For Ngambay, written translation of questionnaires is in testing with our own Ngambay–French model, which we keep improving; a speaker reviews every translation. Speech recognition for Ngambay and Chadian Arabic is in preparation, with data collected only with people's consent.

What happens to the data?

Each organisation sees only its own surveys. Phone numbers are hidden in exports, voice notes are deleted after the period you choose, and reusing them for research needs a second, separate consent.

How can we support SahelVoice?

By sponsoring speech data collection in Chadian Arabic or Ngambay, by running a survey with us, or by becoming a research partner. Write to contact@sahelvoice.app and we will propose the format that suits you.

How much does it cost?

The first pilots in Chad are free, and we set up the survey with you. Write to us to talk about it.
